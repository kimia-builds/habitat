# World-building: N-Z-D

_Kimia's world notes. Not a spec: nothing here changes what Habitat does
yet. It is the reference for designing the regional geographies and,
later, the reading materials. Species names are the same in singular and
plural._

## The planet in brief

- Low gravitational pull. General darkness. Bioluminescent life.
- The ground is not very fertile, so flora tend to float rather than root.
- Wind exists: tendrils and leaves blow around.
- The fauna are well integrated with each other, though species differ in
  number and in kinds of intelligence.
- Literacy, literature and education exist.
- Sixteen regions, each with one native flora.

## Species notes

**1. unif.** The most basic crawling flora on N-Z-D. Occurs in flat
plainlands. Yields fragrant spawn that look like mini stars of itself.

**2. gaba.** A soft, squidgy flora that grows from the ground in
liquid-rich, dewy environments. Yields nutritious edibles that look like
plum-shaped fruit.

**3. simeen.** Floating flora that thrives in a dusty canyon environment.
Its hole catches the dust and its leaves catch starlight; that is how it
survives. Yields teardrop-shaped, colourful leaves.

**4. toom.** Floating flora that combs mist out of the air while it feeds
on airborne microorganisms. Yields organic ribbons, useful for their
purification qualities.

**5. jembesh.** Crawling flora that thrives in pumice and igneous rock.
Yields magical seeds that facilitate spiritual transcendence.

**6. siki.** Grows from the ground in hot, harsh environments, with thorns
to protect itself. Yields protective thorns, good for building protective
shields. The yield looks like a long, thin ribbon covered in spikes, as if
a layer of skin had been peeled off the plant.

**7. honima.** Grows and thrives in rivers of air and doesn't need much to
survive. Yields peace branches: a simpler, smaller version of itself.
Symbolic of peace and friendship because of its bendy but robust quality
(like banana leaves).

**8. bumyaab.** Thrives in calm, shallow liquids, where it rolls around.
Yields juicy treats that look like khormaloos.

**9. zecht.** Found in a sticky bog environment; a slimy flora. Yields
scented biomes that attract friendship.

**10. pataya.** Lives in high canyons and on cliffs, soaking up lots of
starlight. Yields beautiful seasonal blossoms that resemble little
lily-flowered tulips.

**11. budir.** One of the few flora that can survive cold conditions, among
very spiky, vertical ice formations. The budir is slimy and soft, but when
its tendrils are kept dry they produce pretty barbed decoratives.

**12. hayunti.** Attaches to other flora with tendrils through its hole,
and catches mist through its fan system for sustenance. Yields long, thin
strands of edible fruit, like ultra-slim bananas.

**13. duripila.** Thrives in windy environments as a floating flora.
Considered among the most beautiful flora on N-Z-D. Sometimes yields
tendrils that are said to bring good luck.

**14. gubl.** Thrives in frothy liquid and gas-filled mud pots. Yields
reproductive baubles that can be grown at home or played with like a toy.

**15. dalat.** Lives in thick liquids such as oils, in hot environments. Is
hard and crunchy to touch. Yields tropical mist so thick you can carry it
home with you.

**16. kiyajong-ha.** Thrives in complicated environments with high
competition from other flora (the N-Z-D equivalent of a rainforest).
Yields berries for eating.

## Geography clues (a first reading, Claude's — not yet Kimia's)

What each species' lore already says about the land it needs. These are
the raw material for the regional geographies.

| # | Species | Land it asks for | Where its food comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | unif | flat plainlands | the ground (crawls) |
| 2 | gaba | dewy, liquid-rich ground | liquid / dew |
| 3 | simeen | dusty canyon | dust (caught in its hole) and starlight |
| 4 | toom | misty air | airborne microorganisms in mist |
| 5 | jembesh | pumice and igneous rock (volcanic) | rock (crawls on it) |
| 6 | siki | hot, harsh ground | the ground, defended by thorns |
| 7 | honima | rivers of air | very little; the airstream |
| 8 | bumyaab | calm, shallow liquid | the liquid |
| 9 | zecht | sticky bog | the bog |
| 10 | pataya | high canyons and cliffs | starlight |
| 11 | budir | cold, with spiky vertical ice | not stated |
| 12 | hayunti | on other flora | mist, caught by its fan |
| 13 | duripila | windy air | not stated |
| 14 | gubl | frothy liquid, gas-filled mud pots | liquid and gas |
| 15 | dalat | thick oils, hot | the oils |
| 16 | kiyajong-ha | crowded, competitive jungle | not stated |

Patterns worth noticing:

- **Two canyon flora** (simeen low and dusty, pataya high and starlit) and
  **two mist flora** (toom floating, hayunti hanging on others). Each pair
  needs to be told apart by the land, not just the flora.
- **Hot places:** siki (hot, harsh ground), jembesh (volcanic rock) and
  dalat (hot oils), plus gubl's mud pots. Cold appears once, budir, so the
  ice region is the planet's only cold place so far.
- **Liquids come in many kinds:** dew (gaba), calm shallows (bumyaab), bog
  (zecht), froth (gubl), oil (dalat). Five different wet regions, so each
  liquid will need its own character.
- **Air regions:** toom's mist, honima's airstreams, duripila's wind.
- **Fertility is thin everywhere:** most of these live off liquid, dust,
  mist, rock or starlight rather than soil. Only kiyajong-ha implies a
  place rich enough to be crowded.
