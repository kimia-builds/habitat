# World-building: N-Z-D

_Kimia's world notes. Not a spec: nothing here changes what Habitat does
yet. It is the reference for designing the regional geographies and,
later, the reading materials. Species names are the same in singular and
plural._

The notes live in two files, and nothing is written in both:

- **This file** holds what is true across the whole planet.
- **`nzd-regions.xlsx`** holds what is true of one region and its patron
  flora: one row per region, in discovery order, one column per attribute
  (richness, canopy, climate, light, landform, geology, rhythm, the
  liquid, air, gas, how the patron lives, its yield, other flora present).
  It can be filtered and sorted, for example by liquid colour. A blank
  cell means nothing has been decided yet.

## The planet

### Size, liquid and light

- A small planet. Its whole surface is about the size of one country on
  Earth (no finer figure is specified).
- Liquid exists as mist and vapour, and as bodies of liquid in or coming
  out of the ground: lakes, swamps, rivers and some larger seas. There are
  no massive oceans.
- Mist is almost everywhere, especially in humid areas.
- No nearby star, so it is a perpetual starry night. There is no day and
  night.
- Light comes from starlight, bioluminescent life and glowing matter:
  luminous liquids (in many colours), ice and some rocks. Inhabitants are
  barely affected by light levels.
- Reflection nebulae are an optional player setting. They have no lore, no
  regions and no timings attached.

### Heat, gas and air

- A thick, humid atmosphere acts as a blanket and traps warmth. The
  average condition is a swampy, humid heat of about 26°C.
- Heat is mostly geothermal and volcanic. It can also come from dense
  gases and atmospheric pressure.
- Cold is rare. It happens where the blanket is thin or missing, or where
  there is little geothermal heat.
- Dry places are rare too. They are places whose liquids barely evaporate
  (for example thick, oily liquids), so there is little humidity. Dry
  allows cold but does not force it: a dry place can still be hot.
- Some gases are toxic to some species.
- Snow falls in some places. What it is made of is not known in-world.
- Gravity is low. Things float through three mechanisms at once: a thick
  atmosphere, lift gas inside bodies, and gas rising from the ground.
- Creatures' ability to float back down to the ground is left unexplained
  on purpose. Some flora attach to other things to stop floating.
- There is no moon, no reverse gravity and no tides. Waters are calm.
- The player's survival on this planet runs on fictional licence. The
  physics need not be survivable by a human.

### The ground and where life comes from

- The ground is hostile and relatively infertile compared with Earth soil,
  yet it is the ultimate source of all life, as the sun is on Earth: the
  engine under everything, and not a place to live beside. Things still
  live and die on the ground.
- Geology (vents, geysers, mud pots, volcanic activity) supplies heat,
  rising gas, lift, liquid and wind. The sky's food chain starts there.
- Dead floating flora leave the planet as debris and take their nutrients
  with them. This is why the ground is relatively infertile, and why flora
  tend to float rather than root.
- Richness runs from the air or atmosphere (richest), to liquid (mist and
  vapour, then lakes, swamps, rivers and seas), to the ground.

### Liquids

- Waters are calm, so each body of liquid keeps its own chemical character.
  Liquids can differ in density, texture, feel, how and when they
  evaporate, how and when they freeze, how they smell and, most important
  for the map, what colour they take.

### Wind

- Wind has many causes: heat, gas mixing, atmospheric breathing, and
  others. Tendrils and leaves blow around in it.
- Inhabitants perceive and anticipate wind far better than humans do, and
  use it as an everyday tool, much as birds use wind or sea creatures use
  currents. Nobody knows why or how.

## Time and rhythm

- The human idea of linear time does not really exist on N-Z-D. There are
  rhythms instead, and they stand in for the cycles we know (years,
  seasons, lunar cycles, tides, day and night). They sometimes interrelate
  and sometimes don't. Seasons are lore only and never have consequences
  for gameplay.
- **Short, local pulses:** anything that bubbles (geysers, mud pots,
  volcanic vents, bubbling liquids) pulses on its own timer as pressure
  builds and releases. These organise the rhythm of their area.
- **Atmospheric breathing:** drives humidity and wind cycles, and changes
  in gaseous formation.
- **Wind** is a rhythm indicator in its own right.
- **Long-term seasons (the canopy cycle):**
  1. A supergrowth flora, very old and very large, dies and floats off the
     planet as debris. (The scale difference is like a one-year-old oak
     against a 200-year-old one.)
  2. The skies open and let more light in. Many smaller flora die too,
     because they have lost their resting space, which opens the sky
     further.
  3. The flora population starts to regrow. The older, larger flora take
     some time to choose their next supergrowth candidate, which they
     internally prioritise resources for.
  4. It takes the length of a season to bring that candidate to full size.
  5. Then comes a season at maximum size, when the superflora is at its
     largest and the population thrives, until it dies and the cycle
     restarts.
- Canopy cycles are not planet-wide. They are local to areas, and are
  especially unlikely in the cold and dry parts of the planet.

## Sky flora and canopy

- Older and larger floating flora get better at manipulating the air around
  them, so surviving in the sky costs them less energy. Younger flora often
  rest on them.
- Floating flora act as a pseudo-ceiling and as a weather determinant. When
  they populate and hide the stars, the planet gets darker. When they are
  gone, it is lighter.
- **Local sky flora** float around the same region in a more static
  pattern, often overlooking somewhere like a large swamp or a body of
  water.
- **Roaming sky flora** move constantly around the planet. They avoid only
  regions with less seasonal change and mostly open skies. Their elders are
  less large and their reproduction less predictable, so an elder's death
  has less impact on weather and brightness.
- **Vertical canopy** does not float freely. It is made of flora that
  gather starlight but must attach to something tall, like a cliff, and so
  form canopy walls.
- **Starlight as food** is unusual for flora on N-Z-D. Starlight feeders
  live where floating canopy is thin or absent. Where the sky canopy is
  dense for long periods, they also feed on light emitted by bodies of
  water. When the sky clears they thrive doubly and can blossom.

## Regions, borders and life

- There are sixteen regions, and the order of discovery is decided.
- Each region has a patron flora: the one the player discovers there first
  and the one that thrives there. Another flora can recur in a later region
  because it could also live there, but it is not that region's patron.
  A patron can roam many regions, drift out from a home region, or be at
  home in more than one place.
- Borders can be cliffs, seas, canopy walls or gradual edges. Fixed borders
  mostly bind the ground-bound flora and small fauna.
- Abundance varies by region, from lush to sparse. Lush regions have mist,
  liquid and canopy. Sparse ones are ground-only, dry or cold.
- The region work focuses on flora and geology, and perhaps small fauna.
  The larger, more sophisticated fauna have adapted to live anywhere on
  N-Z-D, much as humans do on Earth, so they are not tied to regions.
- The fauna are well integrated with each other, though species differ in
  number and in kinds of intelligence. Literacy, literature and education
  exist.
